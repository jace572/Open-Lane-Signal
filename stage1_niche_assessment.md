# Stage 1 Niche Assessment

*Prepared October 2026. Candidates: Foundation Repair, Water Damage Restoration, Plumbing (Slab Leak and Repipe).*

Fee basis for ROI math: $3K upfront + $997/month. ROI lines show ticket minus the monthly fee on one booked job per month, before the operator's job costs.

---

```
NICHE: Foundation Repair

DIMENSION ASSESSMENTS:
1. TICKET ECONOMICS: STRONG — National average repair runs about $5,200 (typical $2,200–$8,200), and full piering jobs land at $10K–$40K.
2. MARKET SIZE & SCALABILITY: STRONG — Roughly 15,200 US foundation repair contractors (unverified aggregator figure), clustered in expansive-clay and Sunbelt metros where 50+ prospects per metro is realistic.
3. AGENCY SATURATION: MODERATE — A handful of foundation-specific marketing agencies exist and the large PE platforms spend heavily, but it is not in the HVAC/roofing tier of agency crowding.
4. CLEAR DEMAND PROBLEM: STRONG — Independent shops lean on realtor, inspector, and word-of-mouth referrals, and homeowners delay for months, so lead flow is lumpy.
5. B2C ALIGNMENT: STRONG — Residential homeowners are the dominant buyer and most work is cash-pay, since insurance rarely covers gradual settlement.
6. OWNER-OPERATOR DOMINANCE: MODERATE — Most shops by count are still independent, but 7+ PE platforms (Groundworks/KKR, Solid Ground, AnchorPoint, Vanterra and others) are actively acquiring regional brands.
7. OUTBOUND DATA AVAILABILITY: MODERATE — Google Maps/BBB listings, association member lists (FPA, NFRA), and city permit data in some metros; state licensing for this trade is inconsistent.
8. SMS / PHONE REACHABILITY: MODERATE — Small shops have the owner on the cell; mid-size shops route through a sales coordinator who books inspections.

FIT SUMMARY: STRONG FIT

AVG TICKET: $2,200–$8,200 typical (≈$5,200 average); $10K–$40K for full piering
WHY THEY STRUGGLE WITH LEADS: Demand is triggered by visible symptoms that homeowners ignore until a home sale or a big crack forces action. Smaller shops lack a system to capture people in the "noticing" phase, before they call the big brand.
FACEBOOK ADS FIT: STRONG — Highly visual problem (wall cracks, sticking doors, sloping floors), geo-bounded, and the 40–70 homeowner demographic is heavily on Facebook; "free inspection" offers are a proven pattern.
OUTBOUND DATA: Google Maps and BBB, Foundation Performance Association and similar member directories, municipal structural permits, state contractor registries where they exist.
OWNER-OPERATOR %: Est. ~70–80% independent by business count; lower by revenue share due to regional PE-backed brands.
GEOGRAPHIC CONCENTRATION: Texas, Oklahoma, Southeast, Gulf Coast, Midwest clay belts, Colorado Front Range.
ROI LOGIC: $5,200 avg ticket − $997/month = $4,203 on one job/month (first month: $5,200 − $3,997 = $1,203).
COMPETITIVE SATURATION: MEDIUM
OVERALL FIT SCORE: 8

WHAT TO PLAN FOR: Prospect lists are best built in clay-soil metros where independents are dense. In markets where a PE platform is active, the pool of independent shops is smaller, so targeting should be tighter.
```

```
NICHE: Water Damage Restoration

DIMENSION ASSESSMENTS:
1. TICKET ECONOMICS: STRONG — Average residential job is about $3,833 (typical $1,364–$6,301), and average insurance water claims run roughly $12K–$14K.
2. MARKET SIZE & SCALABILITY: STRONG — IBISWorld counts roughly 57K–62K US damage restoration businesses (a broader category that includes fire and mold).
3. AGENCY SATURATION: WEAKER — Restoration marketing is a crowded vertical with many restoration-only agencies, pay-per-call networks, and lead resellers.
4. CLEAR DEMAND PROBLEM: MODERATE — The pain is real, but most operators already work plumber, insurance agent, and TPA referral programs plus Google LSA.
5. B2C ALIGNMENT: STRONG — Residential homeowners dominate, though the insurance carrier is usually the payer and commercial work is meaningful.
6. OWNER-OPERATOR DOMINANCE: MODERATE — Franchise presence is heavy (SERVPRO alone has 2,100+ units under Blackstone, plus PuroClean, Paul Davis, Rainbow, 911 Restoration, BELFOR).
7. OUTBOUND DATA AVAILABILITY: STRONG — IICRC certified-firm locator, Google Maps, state mold/contractor licenses in several states, and insurance preferred-vendor lists.
8. SMS / PHONE REACHABILITY: STRONG — It is a 24/7 emergency business and small owners answer their cell around the clock.

FIT SUMMARY: WORKABLE FIT

AVG TICKET: $1,364–$6,301 typical (≈$3,833 average); insurance claims ≈$12K–$14K
WHY THEY STRUGGLE WITH LEADS: Jobs are emergency-driven and won by whoever answers first on Google. Small independents get outbid on LSA and Search ($150–$400+ per lead) by franchises and lead aggregators.
FACEBOOK ADS FIT: WEAKER — A homeowner with a flooded floor searches and calls right away rather than scrolling Facebook. Facebook mostly plays in retargeting, mold, and non-emergency angles, which makes this the hardest fit for a Facebook-Ads-led offer.
OUTBOUND DATA: IICRC Certified Firm locator (public), Google Maps, state licensing (e.g., TX/FL/NY mold licenses), BBB.
OWNER-OPERATOR %: Est. ~50–65% independent; franchise systems account for a large share of locations.
GEOGRAPHIC CONCENTRATION: Nationwide; storm, hurricane, and freeze-prone regions (Gulf Coast, Southeast, Northeast) see the heaviest volume.
ROI LOGIC: $3,833 avg ticket − $997/month = $2,836 on one job/month (first month: $3,833 − $3,997 = −$164, positive from month two).
COMPETITIVE SATURATION: HIGH
OVERALL FIT SCORE: 6

WHAT TO PLAN FOR: Because demand is emergency search intent, the Facebook offer needs non-emergency angles (mold inspection, leak-detection checks, pre-storm prep) to carry the campaign. Expect operators to compare the offer to LSA and referral programs they already run.
```

```
NICHE: Plumbing (Slab Leak and Repipe)

DIMENSION ASSESSMENTS:
1. TICKET ECONOMICS: STRONG — Slab leak repairs average $2,280–$2,500, and whole-house repipes run about $4,500–$15,000, giving a blended ticket of roughly $5K.
2. MARKET SIZE & SCALABILITY: STRONG — Residential plumbing is a ~$130B, 90%+ fragmented market with tens of thousands of shops; dedicated repipe specialists are fewer, but many general plumbers sell this work.
3. AGENCY SATURATION: WEAKER — Plumbing is one of the most agency-saturated home-service verticals.
4. CLEAR DEMAND PROBLEM: MODERATE — Slab leaks arrive as urgent search-driven calls, while repipes are a considered purchase that most plumbers only sell reactively.
5. B2C ALIGNMENT: STRONG — Single-family homeowners are the core buyer for both services.
6. OWNER-OPERATOR DOMINANCE: STRONG — The market remains 90%+ fragmented by business count, even with ~800 HVAC/plumbing/electrical PE acquisitions since 2022.
7. OUTBOUND DATA AVAILABILITY: STRONG — Plumbers hold state licenses in nearly every state (public lookups), and repipe permits are pullable in many cities.
8. SMS / PHONE REACHABILITY: STRONG — Solo and small shops run off the owner's cell; dispatchers appear only at larger shops.

FIT SUMMARY: WORKABLE FIT

AVG TICKET: $2,280–$2,500 (slab leak); $4,500–$15,000 (repipe); ≈$5K blended
WHY THEY STRUGGLE WITH LEADS: Small shops get emergency calls but rarely market the high-ticket repipe proactively, so the best-margin job depends on whoever happens to call in.
FACEBOOK ADS FIT: MODERATE — Repipes suit Facebook well (homes of a certain age, polybutylene/galvanized/copper pinhole leaks, water-quality and financing angles); slab leaks lean toward search.
OUTBOUND DATA: State plumbing license databases, municipal plumbing permits, Google Maps, BBB.
OWNER-OPERATOR %: Est. 85–90%+ independent by count.
GEOGRAPHIC CONCENTRATION: Slab-on-grade Sunbelt markets (TX, FL, AZ, Southern CA, NV) for slab leaks; older-housing metros (polybutylene 1978–1995 builds, galvanized pre-1960) for repipes.
ROI LOGIC: ≈$5,000 blended ticket − $997/month = $4,003 on one job/month (first month: $5,000 − $3,997 = $1,003).
COMPETITIVE SATURATION: HIGH
OVERALL FIT SCORE: 7

WHAT TO PLAN FOR: The campaign works best positioned on the repipe as a considered, Facebook-friendly purchase rather than on emergency slab calls. Agency competition means operators have heard pitches before, so a niche-specific angle differentiates the outreach.
```

---

## Ranking

1. **Foundation Repair (8/10).** Highest ticket, cash-pay buyer, and the most visual, Facebook-native problem of the three. Weaker spots are inconsistent licensing data and PE consolidation in some metros.
2. **Plumbing, Slab Leak and Repipe (7/10).** Strong ticket, very fragmented owner base, and excellent license data. The repipe side is a good Facebook fit, but agency saturation in plumbing is high.
3. **Water Damage Restoration (6/10).** Strong economics and reachability, but emergency search intent makes Facebook the hardest channel fit, and franchise and agency density is high.

> **All three niches can work.** The scores above reflect operational fit. They help you understand where each niche will need extra attention. The choice is yours; consider personal interest, geographic match, and existing experience alongside these scores.
