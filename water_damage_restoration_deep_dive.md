| Class 1 / Class 2 / Class 3 | $150-$400 / $500-$1,000 / $1,000-$3,000 | [HomeServe](https://www.homeserve.com/blog/cost-guide/water-damage-restoration) and cost guides above |
| Class 4 or flood | $20,000 to $100,000 | One consumer guide in the [cost-guide set](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/disaster-recovery/repair-water-damage/) |

Mitigation is billed separately from repairs. Rebuild items such as drywall and flooring add on top ([Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/articles/water-damage-restoration-cost/)).

**Weighted average ticket (est.)**

| Job mix | Share | Avg ticket | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor (Cat 1, Class 1-2) | 40% | $1,000 | $400 |
| Standard mitigation | 35% | $3,800 | $1,330 |
| Major mitigation (Cat 2-3, multi-room) | 15% | $9,000 | $1,350 |
| Mitigation plus rebuild | 10% | $20,000 | $2,000 |
| **Blended** | | | **about $5,000 (est.)** |

**Retainer math.** Even the minor tier (about $1,000) sits at the planning floor. One standard mitigation job (about $3,800) covers the $997/month fee several times over on revenue. The $3K setup is roughly one major job. The retainer clears comfortably at this AOV, though mitigation margin (not revenue) is the true test.

## 3. Solo Operator Economics

All volume and take-home figures are estimates for a 1-4 truck independent.

| Metric | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Jobs per month | 12-30 mitigation jobs (est.), with a few converting to rebuild |
| Gross revenue | $50K to $120K/month (est., about $5K blended ticket) |
| Gross margin | Industry average about 50%, net about 14%, overhead about 36% ([magicplan 2024 cost-of-doing-business report](https://blog.magicplan.app/2024-cost-of-doing-business-report)). Mitigation runs higher, with sources ranging from 50-80% ([Docusketch](https://docusketch.com/how-much-do-restoration-companies-make)). Reconstruction runs 10-40%. |
| Net margin | 10-15% (est.), with 7% of companies unprofitable per the [R&R summary of the RIA survey](https://www.randrmagonline.com/articles/91677-inside-the-cost-of-doing-business-survey-what-it-means-for-restoration-companies-today) |
| Owner take-home | About $100K to $200K/year (est., owner often works in the field) |

**Receivables.** Contractors often fund jobs 45+ days before final payment ([R&R](https://www.randrmagonline.com/articles/88321-ways-to-avoid-accounts-receivable-battles-with-insurance-carriers)). Mortgage-company co-signed checks can add weeks or months ([Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/blog/the-payment-perils-of-property-restoration-companies/)). One restoration firm reported about 90 days DSO before changing its process ([Levelset case study](https://www.levelset.com/blog/how-a-restoration-company-got-their-payment-cycle-down-to-17-days/), a single vendor case). Cash timing, not margin, is the usual constraint for small operators, and the retainer competes with payroll and equipment for the same cash.

## 4. Lead Acquisition Channels (Ranked by Primacy)

| Rank | Channel | CPL / cost benchmark | Where leads die |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Insurance, TPA, plumber, agent, property-manager referrals | $25-$50 equivalent ([PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/restoration-company-seo/lead-generation/)) | Gated by relationships and program enrollment. Slow to build. |
| 2 | Google Local Services Ads | $80-$180, average $156 (range $83-$390) ([Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/water-damage-restoration-ad-creative-statistics), vendor-reported) | Disputed or out-of-area calls, no credit for some "not serviced" leads, price shoppers |
| 3 | Google Search Ads | $100-$300 per lead ([PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/restoration-company-seo/lead-generation/)). Water damage clicks reported at $91-$251. | Click costs dominate. A missed call in the first minutes sends the caller to the next listing. |
| 4 | Pay-per-call and lead aggregators | Typically sold per call or lead (no verified benchmark found) | Shared leads, speed-to-lead competition |
| 5 | **Facebook / Meta Ads** | $30-$110 per lead for restoration generally ([PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/restoration-company-seo/lead-generation/), vendor-reported) | Low-intent form fills, no contact on first attempts, no response in the first hour |

Leads die at three points. First, the unanswered emergency call goes to a competitor. Second, insurance-funded callers who ask "will insurance cover it" get a vague answer. Third, slow follow-up on form leads. The last two are where the Voice AI and customer SMS back-end carries the most weight.

## 6. Operator Profile and Outreach Reachability

- **Age.** No restoration-specific owner-age dataset was found. As a proxy, US small business owners are about 47% Gen X and 37% boomers ([Oberlo](https://oberlo.com/statistics/average-age-of-small-business-owners)). Likely mid-40s to 60 (est.).
- **Tech and marketing maturity.** Typically LOW to MODERATE (est.). Most use job-management software, but many rely on referrals, a website and a Google profile. Trade-press commentary notes that 62% of restoration firms cannot calculate job-level margin ([RIA study as summarized by the magicplan report](https://blog.magicplan.app/2024-cost-of-doing-business-report)).
- **Willingness to invest in paid ads.** MODERATE. Operators already buy LSA and search leads at $80-$300, so paying for leads is familiar. A $3K plus $997/month commitment is easier to justify against a $3,800 average ticket.
- **SMS reachability (outreach channel).** MODERATE to STRONG for deliverability, since the cell is the business line. Cold B2B texting carries compliance planning needs: carrier 10DLC registration, opt-out handling, and TCPA exposure of $500-$1,500 per violation, with court treatment of business numbers inconsistent ([Tomba](https://tomba.io/blog/cold-texting), [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-texting), [FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/sms-outbound-b2b)). These are vendor blogs and were not checked against FCC or CTIA primary documents. Restoration owners answering at all hours supports fast text response.

## 7. Facebook Ads Channel Fit (Primary Section)

**Does Facebook work?** In part. Demand is split into two types.
- **Emergency demand** (burst pipe, active flooding) is captured by Google search and LSA, because the searcher is actively looking. The vendor behind the $30-$110 range itself says Facebook suits awareness and retargeting better than urgent calls ([PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/restoration-company-seo/lead-generation/)).
- **Non-emergency demand** (moisture checks, mold concerns, leak suspicion, pre-storm prep, claim questions) can be created on Facebook. This is where evidence points ([Slamdot](https://www.slamdot.com/blog/why-social-media-is-great-for-generating-water-damage-and-mold-restoration-leads/), [ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-mold-remediation/)).

No independent case study of a restoration operator running Meta ads for mold or leak offers was found. The evidence is vendor-reported, so the angle ranking below is a judgment (est.).

**CPL benchmarks.** $30-$110 for restoration (vendor-reported, above). One vendor lists $40-$70 for mold, marked low confidence ([ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-mold-remediation/)). A vendor-reported minimum viable budget is $1,500-$3,000/month per platform, and algorithm learning takes about 2-3 weeks ([vendor source](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-water-damage-restoration/)). At a 15-25% lead-to-booked rate (est.), cost per booked job is about $120-$730 against a $3,800 average ticket.

**Top 3 creative angles (ranked by evidence strength, which is limited)**

| # | Angle | Why it fits | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free moisture / mold inspection (non-emergency) | Low-friction offer. Mold surfaces during home sales and after past leaks. | Listed as a proven archetype and a use case ([Slamdot](https://www.slamdot.com/blog/why-social-media-is-great-for-generating-water-damage-and-mold-restoration-leads/), [ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-mold-remediation/)) |
| 2 | Local proof: before/after carousel and owner-on-camera video, with fast-response messaging | Builds trust, then supports retargeting to later emergency searches | Vendor-reported lower CPL for carousels, single-agency ([ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-mold-remediation/)) |
| 3 | Insurance-claim education ("what a policy covers, what to do first") lead magnet with retargeting | Addresses the top objection. Retargeting needs an audience first. | Retargeting recommended widely, evidence thin, and one agency sells it only once traffic is sufficient ([ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-mold-remediation/)) |

Leak detection and pre-storm prep are secondary angles, with less direct evidence.

**Offer architecture (est., descriptive)**

| Layer | Offer |
|---|---|
| Front-end loss leader | Free or low-cost ($99-$149, est.) moisture and mold assessment |
| Core ticket | Extraction and structural drying, about $3,800 average |
| On-site upsell | Mold testing and remediation, contents, rebuild (mitigation 50-80% gross margin versus rebuild 10-40%, [Docusketch](https://docusketch.com/how-much-do-restoration-companies-make)) |
| Recurring | Annual moisture or pre-storm check, plumber and property-manager referral loop |

**Audience parameters (est.).** 10-25 mile service radius. Homeowners, ages roughly 35-65, with broad targeting and the creative doing the segmenting. Storm-event geo bursts after weather alerts. Retargeting pools: video viewers, page and site visitors, form-opens. Lookalikes from past customers once there are enough of them. Meta's lead form captures phone first, to feed missed-call recovery.

## Fit Summary

**Workable Fit.** Retainer math is STRONG (AOV about $3,800 to $5,000, est.), outreach reachability by SMS is MODERATE to STRONG, and the Facebook channel fit is MODERATE: strongest for non-emergency inspection offers and retargeting, weaker for urgent calls.

**Planning notes**
1. **Insurance and TPA dynamics.** Much of the revenue flows through carrier programs and referral networks, and Facebook leads are largely retail, so the front-end offer matters. Receivables of 45-90 days (see Section 3) shape how much cash an operator can commit to the retainer.
2. **Saturation and seasonality.** Google clicks at $91-$251 and LSA costs rising mean operators already feel heavy ad costs, which makes a $30-$110 Facebook CPL attractive on paper. Demand spikes with storms and freezes, so budget pacing by season and weather is part of the campaign plan.
3. **Outreach compliance.** Cold SMS to operator cell phones carries TCPA and 10DLC planning needs (Section 6). PE rollup activity also means some prospects are in sale conversations or already acquired.
