# Foundation Repair: Business Model Deep Dive

Scope: residential piering, underpinning, slab and pier-and-beam leveling, crack repair, with basement waterproofing only where it overlaps. Figures marked (est.) are estimates. Where no primary source was reachable, the figure is labeled as a vendor, broker, or aggregator number. Several vendor pages (for example ClicksGeek and Valley Marketing Group) could not be opened directly, so their figures are quoted from search excerpts only.

## 2. Service Tiers and AOV

Pricing sources are consumer cost guides, which are directional. Per-pier cost is [$1,000 to $3,000](https://www.key.com/to/fwhub+hafoundations). Other guides run to $3,500 and higher, up to $6,000 per pier in complex conditions.

| Tier | Typical price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Crack repair, minor stabilization | ~$500 to $5,000 | Reported [$5,166 average repair, range $2,220 to $8,113](https://www.key.com/to/fwhub+hafoundations). |
| Polyurethane or slab leveling per location | $500 to $1,500 | Per [CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/buy-a-business/foundation-repair/). Faster, thinner margin. |
| Push or helical pier job (4 to 6 piers) | $6,000 to $16,000 | Consumer guide range (est.). |
| Larger pier job (8 to 12 piers) | $15,000 to $40,000 | Consumer guide range (est.). |
| Full engagement (piers plus drainage, moisture control, structural carpentry) | $10,000 to $30,000+ | Pier-and-beam jobs can reach this range. |

Weighted average ticket (est.): about $7,000 to $10,000 for an independent operator. This is a blend of roughly 40% small jobs ($1,500 to $4,000), 40% mid jobs ($8,000 to $14,000), and 20% large jobs ($18,000+). It is an analyst estimate, not a sourced figure. Consumer cost guides put the average single repair closer to $5,200, which is a conservative floor for planning.

Retainer math: at roughly $3K upfront plus $997/month, one incremental booked job at the estimated average ticket covers the monthly fee about 7 to 10 times over on revenue. The niche sits well above the roughly $1K AOV planning floor. Even a small crack or leveling job can cover the monthly fee.

## 3. Solo Operator Economics (est.)

No audited benchmark exists. The inputs below come from broker and buyer-guide material, so this is a modeled range.

| Metric | Estimated range for a 1 to 4 person crew | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly booked jobs | 6 to 15 | Estimate. Pier jobs take 1 to 3 days. |
| Gross revenue | $50K to $140K/month | Jobs times the weighted ticket. |
| Gross margin | 40% to 60% | [Helical 45% to 60%, push 40% to 55%](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/buy-a-business/foundation-repair/). Brokers cite higher figures for subcontracted models. |
| Net margin | 12% to 25% | A [model-based 15% to 25%](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/profitability/house-leveling). A [Denver operator at about 23% cash flow](https://joinaccredited.com/deals/denver-foundation-repair-general-contractor-17-year-colorado-operator) is one data point. | Owner take-home | ~$90K to $250K/year (est.) | Derived from the net margin on roughly $0.7M to $1.7M in annual revenue. |

Planning note: sales-cycle length and engineering-report requirements make monthly volume lumpy, so cash flow tends to follow close rates more than lead counts.

Where leads die (analyst observations, not sourced):
- **Speed to first contact.** Homeowners often request quotes from 2 to 4 companies, and the first live response tends to win the inspection. Missed calls while crews are on site are the main leak, which is where the Voice AI and customer SMS back-end applies.
- **Inspection scheduling and no-shows.** The free inspection is the conversion gate.
- **Price shock.** Homeowners who expected $1K to $3K meet a $10K+ scope, so financing and engineer-report framing matter.
- **Sticker-shock stall.** Follow-up cadence after the quote is where many booked jobs are won back.

## 5. Owner-Operator vs. Consolidation

No authoritative split exists. Based on the fragmented structure (10,000+ firms against a handful of platforms), independents plausibly make up 85% to 90% or more of firms by count (est.). Franchise and dealer networks (Ram Jack, Supportworks) are better described as affiliated independents. PE-backed platforms hold a meaningful but minority revenue share, concentrated in Texas, the Southeast, the Mid-Atlantic, and the Midwest.

Active 2025 to 2026 rollups (verified through deal announcements):
- **Groundworks (KKR).** Acquired [AquaGuard](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/gaS8Y8). Press coverage also reports a Houston deal (Leveled Concrete, September 2025) and WTX Foundation Repair (February 2026), which came from aggregator summaries and were not confirmed against the primary releases.
- **Solid Ground Solutions (CenterOak).** [Platform formed January 2025](https://markets.financialcontent.com/concordmonitor/article/bizwire-2025-1-9-centeroak-partners-forms-foundation-repair-services-platform), Columbus, Ohio, with add-ons planned.
- **Vanterra (Percheron).** Added [58 Foundations & Waterproofing (Knoxville)](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/LXSZMX) in January 2025.
- **AnchorPoint (Oridian).** [Launched June 2026](https://www.mdm.com/news/top-distributor-sectors/building-materials-construction/oridian-capital-launches-foundation-repair-waterproofing-platform-anchorpoint/) with five initial brands. AnchorPoint is a separate platform and does not include Solid Ground.

Operational implication: exit valuations of [4x to 9x EBITDA](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/buy-a-business/foundation-repair/) mean some independents are already being approached by buyers. In markets with an active platform, outreach can verify whether a prospect has recently sold or signed a letter of intent.

## 6. Operator Profile and Outreach Reachability

All items below are qualitative (est.) and not backed by a survey.

| Dimension | Observation |
|---|---|
| Owner age | Skews 40 to 60 (est.). Many are former crew members, estimators, or installers who started a firm. |
| Tech sophistication | Moderate. Uses a cell phone, a basic CRM or estimating tool, and Google Business Profile. |
| Marketing maturity | Low to moderate. Typically relies on referrals, a website, and perhaps one lead-vendor relationship. |
| Willingness to invest in paid ads | MODERATE to STRONG. High tickets make paid spend easy to justify, and many already pay for LSA. |
| SMS reachability for outbound pitching | MODERATE to STRONG. The cell phone is typically the business line, listed on the website and Google profile. Larger shops route calls through office staff, so SMS lands better with the 1 to 4 person ICP. |

Outreach (SMS) and the offer (Facebook Ads management) are scored separately. Weaker SMS reachability in a given market would call for an alternate outreach channel, and the offer itself would be unaffected.

## 7. Facebook Ads Channel Fit (Primary Section)

**Does Facebook work?** Yes, with a specific role. Foundation repair is a need-driven purchase, and most homeowners do not browse for it. One agency source describes Facebook as [not the primary lead channel but the best way to warm top-of-funnel audiences and lower Google CPL](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-foundation-repair/) (agency source, excerpt only). The evidence for Facebook as a standalone primary channel is thin. The case for it rests on three things: a $7K to $10K estimated ticket, a visible symptom set (cracks, sticking doors, sloping floors) that homeowners can recognize in a creative, and an audience of older homeowners who are active on the platform. Proof of performance in this niche would come from early campaigns, since public foundation-specific benchmarks are limited.

**CPL benchmarks.**
- Cross-industry Facebook lead ads: [$27.66 average (2025)](https://prospeo.io/s/average-cost-per-lead-by-industry); other aggregated ranges are $28 to $50.
- Foundation-specific estimate: $40 to $120 per form lead, with a lower-intent lead requiring more qualification (est.).
- Comparison to Google LSA at [$60 to $75](https://thevalleymarketinggroup.com/blog/foundation-repair-google-ads-cost-per-lead-2026/). Facebook leads are cheaper per lead but convert at a lower rate because intent is created, not captured. The more useful metric is cost per booked inspection and cost per signed job.
- Illustrative math (est.): at $80 CPL, a 25% lead-to-inspection rate, and a 30% inspection-to-close rate, cost per job is about $1,070, against a ~$7K to $10K ticket.

**Top 3 winning creative angles** (analyst synthesis of common patterns in the trade, not tested data):
1. **Symptom recognition.** Real photos of stair-step cracks, sticking doors, and sloping floors with a "Is this serious?" hook. It speaks to homeowners who have noticed a symptom and have not acted.
2. **Free inspection plus engineer-style assessment.** A no-pressure inspection with a written report lowers the commitment barrier and positions the operator against high-pressure national sales teams.
3. **Local proof and warranty.** Before and after jobs, the owner on camera, a transferable warranty, and local review counts. Independents can contrast with PE-backed brands on accountability.

**Recommended offer architecture** (descriptive of how the niche tends to stack):

| Layer | Offer |
|---|---|
| Front-end loss leader | Free foundation inspection, or a low-priced inspection with a written report that credits toward the job. |
| Core ticket | Pier or leveling job at $6K to $16K, financing offered. |
| On-site upsell | Drainage, gutter and grading correction, crawl space moisture control, concrete leveling, and waterproofing where it overlaps. |
| Recurring play | Annual monitoring or inspection plan, transferable warranty registration, and referral incentives to realtors and neighbors. |

**Audience targeting parameters** (starting points, to be validated):
- Homeowners aged 40 to 70 within a 15 to 30 mile service radius.
- Home age of roughly 20+ years where the platform allows proxies; zip codes selected by soil type (expansive clay, high water table) and housing stock, such as pier-and-beam neighborhoods in Texas.
- Broad targeting with strong creative tends to outperform narrow interest stacks under current Meta delivery (general practitioner view, not sourced).
- Retargeting video viewers and website visitors; lookalikes from past customers.
- Lead form with 3 to 5 fields plus a qualifying question (symptom type, owner or renter) to reduce unqualified submissions.
- Backstopped by Voice AI missed-call recovery and customer SMS follow-up, since speed to first contact is the main place leads are lost.

## Fit Summary

**Overall: Workable Fit to Strong Fit.**

| Dimension | Rating |
|---|---|
| AOV versus retainer | STRONG |
| Facebook Ads channel fit | MODERATE (a demand-creation channel with a high ticket) |
| SMS outreach reachability | MODERATE to STRONG |
| Market fragmentation | STRONG |

**Planning notes**
1. **Consolidation and geography.** Platform activity is heaviest in Texas, the Southeast, the Mid-Atlantic, and Ohio. Markets with several roll-ups bidding for leads can face higher Google costs and more buyer interest in independents. Markets in clay-soil regions have the deepest demand.
2. **Seasonality and sales cycle.** Foundation issues surface in dry-spell and wet-season swings, and winter can slow exterior work in northern states. Higher-ticket scopes also bring longer decisions and a financing step. A 30 to 60 day close cycle is typical.
3. **Regulatory and data quality.** Licensing, engineering requirements, and warranty terms vary by state. Market-size and CPL data in the public domain are thin and conflicting, so early campaign results will be the real benchmark.
