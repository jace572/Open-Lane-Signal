# Niche Validation and Bible Build — Workflow Rules

Rules that apply when generating any output inside this workflow (validation assessments, deep dives, offer banks, niche bibles, ad copy). Read these before producing member-facing artifacts.

---

## 1. Two-channel architecture: SMS outreach, Facebook Ads offer

The go-to-market has two distinct channels that must stay separate in any research, copy, or assessment:

- **OUTREACH channel = SMS** — cold pitch sent to SMB operator cell phones. This is how the member acquires new clients. **SMS reachability of operators is load-bearing for outreach** — if you can't reach the owner on a cell, you can't pitch.
- **OFFER channel = Facebook Ads management** — the headline product the member sells (creative, targeting, funnel build, ongoing optimization). Once an operator is signed, FB Ads runs regardless of how they were acquired.
- **Back-end stack delivered to the operator** = FB Ads + Voice AI (call answering, missed-call recovery) + customer SMS (qualification, follow-up). The full service includes Voice AI and customer SMS, but they're back-end infrastructure for the operator's lead handling — **not the headline pitch**.

**How to apply:**
- In niche assessments and deep dives, score outreach reachability and Facebook Ads channel fit as **separate dimensions**. Don't merge them.
- Watch for "Voice AI is the wedge" / "SMS-first lead gen" framing — that conflates the operator's back-end stack with the member's go-to-market. Reframe.
- Lead all sales/marketing copy with "Facebook Ads management" as the headline offer. Mention Voice AI / customer SMS only as part of the delivered stack.
- A niche where SMS reachability is weak isn't disqualified — it just means the member needs a different outreach channel (cold call, email, in-person) for that niche. The Facebook Ads offer still works.

---

## 2. Member-facing decision materials use objective gradient scoring, not fear language

When a member is choosing between options (niches, offers, strategies), the materials must provide **objective operational data** without dramatic verdict language. The member's autonomy to choose — informed by the data plus personal factors — must be preserved.

**Forbidden language in member-facing decision materials:** FAIL, KILL, ABORT, DO NOT PROCEED, HARD FAIL, "kill signal" — even in negation ("doesn't kill the niche"). Always rephrase positively.

**Replace with:**
- PASS/FAIL → STRONG / MODERATE / WEAKER, or Strong Fit / Workable Fit / Requires More Effort
- Verdict labels (PROCEED / DO NOT PROCEED) → descriptive summaries of what the data means operationally
- Warnings about weaker dimensions → planning notes ("plan for extra effort here")

**Always include a closing line affirming all options can work** — the data shows fit, not validity. The member chooses based on operational data, personal interest, geographic match, and existing experience.

**Why:** Sam pre-screens the options he gives members. If three niches make it onto a shortlist, all three can work. Heavy verdict language pigeonholes members into the top-ranked option and makes them feel forced rather than informed.

---

## 3. Member-facing deliverables use descriptive voice, not instructional voice

Deliverables (offer banks, niche bibles, competitive intel reports) read as strategic intelligence the member collects and submits, not as instructions for the member to act on. The member submits the deliverable to the campaign team for execution; they shouldn't feel they have homework.

**Cut from deliverables:**
- "How to Use This..." / "Getting Started" / "Step-by-Step" sections
- "Use when:" → replace with "Best for:"
- "Sample Combos to Test" → "Common Combos in This Niche" (describe what's observed, not prescribed)
- "What to Skip" → "Common Pitfalls in This Niche"
- Second-person "your campaigns / your ads" → "the campaign / the niche / operators in this space"

**Keep in deliverables:**
- Strategic context, market observations, examples with attribution, reasoning (why each angle works)
- These are informational, not instructional

**Workflow SOPs** (the docs that operationalize the process) are different — those are instructional toward the member doing the work, and that's appropriate.

---

## 4. ICP is fixed across niches: the SMS-responsive solo / small-multi owner-operator

The Ideal Customer Profile doesn't change per niche. It's always:
- 1–4 truck / 1–4 employee owner-operator
- Cell phone is the business line
- Independent (not franchise, not PE-rollup-acquired)
- Tradesperson scaled into business owner — marketing-unsophisticated

Don't re-derive the ICP per niche. The "operator tiers table" in any deep dive maps the market structure (who exists, fragmentation, franchise risk) — it doesn't pick a buyer. The buyer is fixed.

---

## 5. No em dashes in member-facing copy

Em dashes signal AI-generated writing. Use periods, commas, or sentence restructures instead.

**Exceptions:** the validation prompt's structured output template uses em dashes as separators — that's intentional formatting, leave it. The rule applies to free-flowing copy (offer banks, deep dive prose, ad copy, sales messaging).

---

## 6. Niche viability threshold: AOV ~$1K+ as a planning floor

The retainer math is $3K upfront + $997/month. For one incremental booked job per month to cover the fee with margin, the niche's blended AOV should land **roughly $1K or higher**. Niches with AOV below that floor can still work — they require a higher booked-job volume to clear the retainer — but flag the math explicitly so the member knows what they're committing to.

This is a planning signal, not a gate.

---

## When These Rules Kick In

Apply these rules when generating:
- Validation assessments (Stage 1 outputs)
- Deep dives (Stage 2 outputs)
- Offer banks (Stage 3 outputs)
- Niche bibles (Stage 4 deliverables)
- Any ad copy, sales scripts, or campaign creative produced downstream of this workflow

The rules don't apply to internal SOPs in this folder (those describe the process and are appropriately instructional for whoever's running the workflow).
