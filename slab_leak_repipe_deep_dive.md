# Slab Leak and Repipe: Business Model Deep Dive

Evidence note: published figures for this sub-niche are thin. Most cost and CPL data comes from vendor blogs and cost aggregators with no shared methodology. Items marked (est.) are analyst estimates built from those inputs, not sourced statistics.

## 1. Industry Snapshot

- **Market size:** IBISWorld puts US plumber industry revenue at about [$191.4B in 2026, with a 3.1% five-year CAGR](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/plumbers/1946/). A secondary compilation attributes a different figure to IBISWorld, [$169.8B (2025) and roughly 132,000 businesses](https://fieldcamp.ai/plumbing-software/plumbing-industry-trends-statistics/). IBISWorld's own pages did not display the business count, so the 132,000 figure is unverified, and the two revenue numbers conflict. Census groups plumbing with HVAC under NAICS 23822, which makes counts hard to compare.
- **Growth:** about 3% a year (see the IBISWorld link above). Slab leak and repipe growth is not separately published.
- **Sub-segment size (est.):** There is no authoritative count of repipe specialists. A working estimate is 1,500 to 3,000 US firms that actively market repipe or slab leak work as a core line, which is roughly 1 to 2% of plumbing firms. Many more general plumbers do the work occasionally. The addressable base is housing stock. Polybutylene was [installed in up to 10 million US homes between 1978 and 1995, concentrated in Florida, California and Texas](https://moderncastle.com/polybutylene-pipes/). Copper pinhole leaks are tied to [water chemistry, chloramines and high pressure](https://www.benjaminfranklinplumbing.com/alpharetta/blog/2026/april/alpharetta-homeowners-pinhole-leaks/). Older homes with [galvanized pipe](https://www.thepricer.org/replacing-galvanized-pipes-cost/) form the third driver.
- **Market structure:** [Highly fragmented, with consolidation accelerating](https://www.lek.com/insights/building-construction/tapping-growth-plumbing-services). A franchise-focused analysis says the [vast majority of residential plumbers are independent sole proprietors or small regional firms](https://peersense.com/franchise/mr-rooter-spv-llc) (no precise share given).
- **Top national brands (verified as far as sources allow):**

| Brand | Ownership and notes |
|---|---|
| Roto-Rooter | Public (Chemed). [Buying back franchise territories](https://chemedcorporation.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/roto-rooter-completes-acquisitions-two-significant-territories) |
| Mr. Rooter (Neighborly) | Franchisor claims [230+ locations](https://www.franchising.com/neighborly/) (self-reported) |
| Apex Service Partners | [Alpine Investors platform](https://www.contractormag.com/management/article/20884107/alpine-acquires-frank-gay-services), combined HVAC/plumbing/electrical |
| Sila Services | PE-backed per [this guide](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/guides/private-equity-plumbing-2026/) (single source) |
| Wrench Group | Listed as PE-backed in the same guide. Ownership history is inconsistent across sources |
| Benjamin Franklin (Authority Brands) | Franchise brand. Location counts not verified in this research |
| Repipe Specialists | Not a franchise. An [asset-light lead-gen and project model for partner plumbers](https://www.gryphon-inv.com/companies/repipe-specialists/) (Gryphon-backed) |
| PRS Pipe Restoration Solutions | [Small trenchless and condo pipe restoration firm](https://prospeo.io/c/prs-pipe-restoration-solutions-inc-revenue). No franchise model found |

## 2. Service Tiers and AOV

Ranges below combine [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/plumbing/slab-leak-repair/), [Fixr](https://www.fixr.com/costs/slab-leak-repair), a [Denver-area plumber](https://ai.brothersplumbing.com/blogs/Centennial--comma---CO-Leak-Detection-and-Repair-Costs-%E2%80%94-Plumbing-Guide) and [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/replacing-galvanized-pipes-cost/). Aggregator averages run lower than specialist quotes. The mix and weights are (est.).

| Tier | Typical ticket | Assumed job mix (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| Leak detection only | $150 to $400 | 15% |
| Slab spot repair (through slab) | $500 to $4,000 (avg ~$2,500) | 35% |
| Reroute (overhead or around slab) | $1,500 to $6,000 per the Denver plumber; one 2026 guide shows $4,800 to $14,500 | 20% (~$7,500) |
| Full repipe, PEX | $4,000 to $15,000 | 22% (~$11,000) |
| Full repipe, copper or premium (larger homes, wall restoration) | 30 to 50% above PEX on materials (weak source); $15,000 to $20,000+ | 3% (~$18,000) |

**Weighted average ticket (est.): about $5,400.** This is well above the ~$1K planning floor. Detection-only calls (about $350) are below the floor but usually feed a repair or repipe.

## 3. Solo Operator Economics (all est.)

| Metric | Range |
|---|---|
| Booked jobs per month (1 to 2 trucks) | 10 to 18 |
| Gross revenue per month | $50K to $95K |
| Gross margin (after materials, labor, permits) | 45 to 55% |
| Net margin | 12 to 20% |
| Owner take-home per year | $90K to $160K |

**Retainer math:** First-year cost is $3,000 plus 12 × $997, or about $14,964, excluding ad spend. At ~$5,400 AOV and ~50% gross margin, one job yields roughly $2,700 of gross profit. One incremental booked job per month covers the $997 fee 2.7 times over. The upfront fee is recovered by about 1.1 additional jobs spread across the year.

## 4. Lead Acquisition Channels (ranked by primacy)

Benchmarks are vendor-published. Sources disagree, so they are best read as ranges.

| Rank | Channel | CPL benchmark | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google LSA and Search | LSA [$25 to $75 per lead, avg $40 to $50](https://cdcalculators.com/local-service-ads-calculator/). Search averages [$129 per lead for plumbing in one 2025 benchmark](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/google-ads-for-plumbers/). Older [LocalIQ data shows $48.91](https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-advertising-benchmarks/) | Captures emergency intent ("slab leak repair near me") |
| 2 | Facebook/Meta Ads | [$34 to $58 blended; $95 to $140 for repipe-type campaigns](https://pipelineon.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-plumbers/) | Planned, consideration-stage demand (Section 7) |
| 3 | Angi/Thumbtack | Shared leads, [$40 to $85+ per lead](https://thesearchsherpa.com/plumber-seo-cost/) (via SEO vendor) | Shared leads, price-driven |
| 4 | Insurance, restoration and home-inspector referrals | No published CPL | Relationship-based. Strong for slab leak and polybutylene findings |

**Where leads die:** (1) Speed to contact. Missed calls and slow callbacks are the largest loss point for a 1 to 4 person crew on job sites, which is where back-end missed-call recovery and SMS qualification help. (2) The estimate-to-close step on $8K to $15K repipes, where homeowners compare several quotes and want financing. (3) Instant-form leads, which [cut CPL 30 to 50% but reduce sales conversion 50 to 70%](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/plumbing-facebook-ads-statistics). (4) Insurance coverage uncertainty on slab leaks.

## 7. Facebook Ads Channel Fit (Primary Section)

**Does Facebook work here?** Yes for planned, high-ticket work, and MODERATE for emergency slab leaks. One source notes [Facebook struggles with emergencies and performs better on planned, higher-margin services such as whole-home repipes](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/facebook-ads-for-plumbers/). Emergency slab leak intent sits mostly with Google. Facebook captures homeowners who have not yet searched: those with polybutylene, aging copper or a prior pinhole leak, and those who got a high water bill.

**CPL and cost-per-job benchmarks:**
- Blended plumbing CPL is [$34 to $58, and $95 to $140 for repipe and tankless campaigns](https://pipelineon.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-plumbers/). Cost per booked job is [$280 to $520](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/plumbing-facebook-ads-statistics), versus LSA at $168 in the same source. Another source cites LSA at $120, so vendor figures conflict. Webtonic's table gives cost per click of $0.80 to $1.20.
- Illustrative math (est.): $120 repipe CPL, 25% of leads become booked estimates ($480 per estimate), 35% close ($1,370 per sold job). Against an $11,000 PEX repipe, ad cost is about 12% of revenue.

**Top 3 winning creative angles** (the sources describe general plumbing creative. Mapping to repipe is analyst inference):
1. **Hidden risk awareness.** Polybutylene and pinhole-leak warnings, such as "Built between 1978 and 1995? Many homes still have PB piping, and leaks hide behind walls." Pairs a stamp-code identifier (PB2110) with a free inspection.
2. **Real job-site video and before/after.** A [genuine 30-second job-site video consistently outperforms template creative](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/facebook-ads-for-plumbers/). Examples: a time-lapse PEX repipe, a one-day or two-day completion, minimal drywall damage.
3. **Fixed price and financing.** Upfront pricing, a written guarantee, and monthly payment framing for $8K to $15K projects.

**Recommended offer architecture:**

| Layer | Offer |
|---|---|
| Front-end loss leader | Free or low-cost whole-home pipe assessment or water-pressure and leak check ($0 to $99) |
| Core ticket | Repipe (PEX or copper) or reroute, $4K to $15K+ |
| On-site upsell | Water heater, PRV/pressure regulator, shutoff valves, whole-home filtration, drywall and paint restoration |
| Recurring play | Annual plumbing inspection plan or leak-monitoring device plus a membership, which also feeds referrals |

**Audience targeting parameters:**
- Homeowners (not renters), ages 35 to 70, home value $250K and up where available.
- Home age: built 1978 to 1995 (polybutylene era), pre-1960 (galvanized), and 1980s to 2000s (copper pinhole exposure).
- Geography: radius of 15 to 25 miles around the service area. Texas, Florida, California and Arizona are the priority markets, where slab-on-grade construction and polybutylene concentration overlap. Hard-water and chloramine markets raise pinhole demand.
- Lookalikes from past customer lists, with the Conversions API installed and measurement on cost per booked job. Instant forms with qualifying questions (home year, pipe material) are one way to hold quality.
- Neighborhood-level targeting by subdivision build year using county records.

## Fit Summary

**Workable Fit to Strong Fit.** The AOV (~$5,400 weighted) comfortably clears the retainer math, SMS reachability of operators is STRONG, and Facebook is a good match for planned repipe demand. Emergency slab leak capture is MODERATE on Facebook and leans on Google.

**Planning notes:**
1. **Agency saturation.** Many agencies and vendors already pitch plumbers on Facebook and Google, so operators may have prior agency experience. A repipe-specific offer and cost-per-booked-job reporting differentiate the pitch.
2. **Geography.** Demand tracks housing stock (polybutylene in FL, CA and TX; slab-on-grade in the Sun Belt; hard-water copper markets). Northern markets lean on galvanized and cast-iron work instead. Mapping the operator's service area to home age data helps set expectations.
3. **Licensing and close process.** Plumbing licenses and permits are state and local, and repipe sales often involve in-home estimates, financing and insurance paperwork. Operators with a sales process and financing partner convert better. Back-end missed-call recovery and customer SMS qualification help protect leads during that cycle.
